How Does a Mortgage Calculator Work?

A mortgage calculator estimates the regular payment required to repay a home loan over a specified period. The calculation uses the loan amount, interest rate and loan term.

The loan amount is calculated by subtracting the down payment from the home price. The calculator then applies the assumed interest rate and repayment period to estimate the monthly principal and interest payment.

The results can help you compare different mortgage scenarios and understand how the interest rate, down payment and loan term affect the overall cost of borrowing.

Mortgage Payment Formula

A standard fixed-rate mortgage payment can be calculated using the loan amount, monthly interest rate and number of monthly payments.

The basic formula is:

M = P × [r(1 + r)n] / [(1 + r)n − 1]

In this formula, M is the monthly payment, P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the total number of monthly payments.

This calculator uses that standard amortization formula for loans with a non-zero interest rate. When the interest rate is 0%, the loan is simply divided across the number of monthly payments.

Why Does the Interest Rate Matter?

The interest rate affects how much you pay to borrow money. A higher interest rate generally increases the monthly payment and the total amount of interest paid over the life of the loan.

Even relatively small differences in mortgage rates can result in significant differences in total interest when a loan is repaid over several decades.

How Does the Down Payment Affect a Mortgage?

A larger down payment reduces the amount you need to borrow. This can reduce the monthly mortgage payment and the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

The appropriate down payment depends on your financial situation, available savings, the property and the mortgage terms offered by the lender.

How Does the Loan Term Affect Mortgage Costs?

A longer mortgage term generally reduces the required monthly payment but can increase the total amount of interest paid.

A shorter term can result in higher monthly payments but may reduce the total interest paid because the loan is repaid faster.

Example: $300,000 Home With a $60,000 Down Payment

Suppose you purchase a $300,000 home and make a $60,000 down payment. The estimated mortgage loan would therefore be $240,000.

At an assumed annual interest rate of 6% over 30 years, the estimated monthly principal and interest payment would be approximately $1,438.92.

Over 30 years, the estimated total of these mortgage payments would be approximately $517,971, including approximately $277,971 in interest.

This example is hypothetical. Actual mortgage costs can differ because of the interest rate offered by the lender, loan fees, taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance and other expenses.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is included in a mortgage payment?

This calculator estimates principal and interest. Actual housing costs can also include property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, maintenance and other expenses, which are not included in this calculator.

Does this mortgage calculator include property taxes?

No. This calculator estimates principal and interest only. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance and other additional costs are not included.

How much house can I afford?

A mortgage payment calculator estimates the payment for a loan, but it does not determine how much home you can personally afford. Your income, existing debts, expenses, savings and other financial circumstances also affect an affordable housing budget.

You can use our Mortgage Affordability Calculator to explore this question using income and expense assumptions.

Does a larger down payment reduce interest?

Generally, yes. A larger down payment reduces the amount borrowed, which can reduce the total interest paid over the life of the loan.

Is a 30-year mortgage always cheaper?

A longer loan term can result in a lower monthly payment but may lead to more total interest paid. A shorter term can increase monthly payments while reducing the repayment period.

What happens if mortgage interest rates increase?

For a new fixed-rate mortgage, a higher interest rate generally results in a higher monthly principal and interest payment and higher total interest costs, assuming the loan amount and term remain the same.

Are mortgage calculator results guaranteed?

No. The results are estimates based on the assumptions entered. Actual mortgage costs depend on the lender, interest rate, fees, taxes, insurance and other factors.

Can I use this calculator to compare mortgage scenarios?

Yes. You can change the home price, down payment, interest rate and loan term to compare different hypothetical mortgage scenarios.

Does this calculator include mortgage insurance?

No. Mortgage insurance and other lender-specific costs are not included in the calculation. The calculator focuses on the loan's principal and interest.
