What is net worth?

Net worth is the value of everything you own minus the money you owe. It provides a simple snapshot of your overall financial position at a particular point in time.

Your assets can include cash, savings, investments, property and other valuable possessions. Liabilities include debts such as credit cards, loans and mortgages.

How is net worth calculated?

The basic formula is:

Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities

For example, if you have $50,000 in assets and $20,000 in liabilities, your net worth would be $30,000.

Why calculate your net worth?

Tracking your net worth can help you understand how your financial position changes over time. Updating your numbers periodically can also help you monitor savings, investments and debt.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I include in my net worth?

Include your major assets such as cash, savings, investments and property, as well as your outstanding debts and other liabilities.

Is a negative net worth possible?

Yes. If your total liabilities are greater than your total assets, your net worth will be negative.

How often should I calculate my net worth?

You can calculate it whenever useful to you. Many people choose to review their financial position periodically to track changes in assets and debts.
