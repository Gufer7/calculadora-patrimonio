How does a mortgage affordability calculator work?

A mortgage affordability calculator estimates a potential housing budget using your income and existing monthly debt payments.

This calculator uses a housing payment limit of 28% of gross monthly income. Existing monthly debt payments are then considered separately when estimating the potential mortgage amount.

The result is an estimate rather than a lending decision. Actual mortgage approval depends on factors such as credit history, employment, assets, lender requirements, taxes, insurance and other costs.

Why does income matter?

Higher gross monthly income generally allows for a larger potential housing payment under the assumptions used by the calculator.

How do existing debts affect affordability?

Existing debt payments reduce the amount of your income available for housing and can affect the mortgage amount a lender may consider affordable.

How does the down payment affect affordability?

A larger down payment means you need to borrow less money for the same home price. It can also reduce the loan-to-value ratio of the mortgage.

How does the interest rate affect affordability?

A higher interest rate increases the monthly payment required for the same loan amount. A lower rate can reduce the monthly payment.

Important: This calculator provides an educational estimate only. It does not determine how much a lender will approve. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, maintenance, mortgage insurance and other costs may also affect the amount you can comfortably afford.

Mortgage Affordability Calculator FAQ

How much house can I afford?

The answer depends on your income, existing debts, down payment, interest rate, loan term and other housing costs. Use the calculator above to explore different scenarios.

Does this calculator guarantee mortgage approval?

No. The result is only an estimate. Mortgage lenders use their own underwriting criteria and may consider many additional factors.

Does the calculator include taxes and insurance?

No. The basic calculation focuses on the mortgage payment and does not include property taxes, homeowners insurance, maintenance or other ownership costs.

Can I change the interest rate?

Yes. Enter a different expected annual mortgage interest rate to compare different scenarios.
