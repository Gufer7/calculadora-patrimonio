How Does a Mortgage Affordability Calculator Work?

A mortgage affordability calculator estimates a potential home-buying budget by comparing your gross monthly income, existing debt payments and the estimated cost of a mortgage.

This calculator uses a simplified 28/36 approach. The estimated housing payment is limited to 28% of gross monthly income, while total monthly debt obligations are limited to 36% of gross monthly income under the calculator's assumptions.

These percentages are commonly used as general affordability guidelines, but they are not universal lending requirements. Actual mortgage underwriting can use different criteria.

Why Does Income Matter?

Gross monthly income is one of the main inputs used when estimating mortgage affordability. A higher income generally provides more room for housing payments under a fixed affordability ratio.

How Do Existing Debts Affect Affordability?

Existing monthly debt payments reduce the amount of income available for a new mortgage when using a debt-to-income approach.

For example, car loans, student loans and other qualifying monthly debt payments can reduce the amount of additional housing debt that fits within a target debt-to-income ratio.

How Does the Down Payment Affect Home Affordability?

A larger down payment means less money needs to be borrowed for the same home price. In this calculator, the estimated home price is the maximum estimated mortgage amount plus the down payment entered.

A larger down payment can also reduce the loan-to-value ratio. Lenders may use loan-to-value when evaluating a mortgage and determining whether mortgage insurance or other conditions apply. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

How Does the Interest Rate Affect Affordability?

The mortgage interest rate affects how much you can borrow for a given monthly payment. A higher interest rate generally means that the same monthly payment supports a smaller loan.

A lower interest rate can allow a larger loan for the same monthly payment, assuming the loan term and other assumptions remain unchanged.

Why Does the Loan Term Matter?

A longer loan term spreads repayment over more monthly payments. This can allow a given monthly payment to support a larger loan, although the borrower may pay more interest over the full life of the mortgage.

Important: This calculator provides an educational estimate only. It does not determine how much a lender will approve or how much home you should buy. Actual affordability can also depend on property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA fees, maintenance, closing costs, credit history, assets and other financial circumstances.

28/36 Affordability Rule Explained

The 28/36 approach is a commonly referenced mortgage affordability guideline. The 28% portion compares housing costs with gross monthly income, while the 36% portion considers total monthly debt obligations.

For example, if gross monthly income is $6,000, 28% equals $1,680. If existing monthly debt is $500, a 36% total-debt limit would leave up to $1,660 for the new housing payment.

Because these are simplified guidelines rather than universal lending rules, the result should be treated as a starting point for comparing scenarios rather than a borrowing limit.

Current mortgage underwriting rules can allow different debt ratios depending on the borrower, loan type and underwriting method. For example, Fannie Mae's current guidance includes different maximum DTI limits depending on the underwriting method. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Mortgage Affordability Calculator FAQ

How much house can I afford?

It depends on your income, existing debts, down payment, mortgage rate, loan term and other housing costs. This calculator provides an estimate using simplified affordability assumptions.

What is the 28% rule for buying a house?

The 28% rule is a general guideline suggesting that housing costs should not exceed roughly 28% of gross monthly income. It is a guideline rather than a universal lender requirement. Fannie Mae and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau both reference 28% as a common mortgage affordability benchmark. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

What is the 36% rule?

The 36% rule is a general debt-to-income guideline that considers total monthly debt relative to gross income. Different lenders and mortgage programs may use different thresholds.

Does this calculator guarantee mortgage approval?

No. The result is only an estimate. Lenders can consider credit history, income stability, assets, reserves, property characteristics, loan type and other factors when evaluating a mortgage application. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Does the calculator include property taxes and insurance?

No. The calculator estimates the mortgage payment based on the loan assumptions entered. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA fees and maintenance are not included in the estimated home price.

Actual mortgage underwriting can include housing expenses such as taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance and association dues. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

Can I change the mortgage interest rate?

Yes. Enter a different annual mortgage interest rate to compare how changing the rate affects your estimated loan amount and home price.

Does a larger down payment increase how much house I can afford?

Under this calculator's simplified model, yes. The estimated home price equals the estimated maximum loan plus the down payment entered. A larger down payment can therefore increase the estimated purchase price while reducing the amount borrowed.

Should I buy the most expensive home I can afford?

Not necessarily. A lender's maximum loan amount and a household's comfortable budget are not necessarily the same thing. Other expenses, savings goals, emergencies, maintenance and future changes in income can affect what is financially comfortable. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

What debts should I include?

Enter recurring monthly debt payments such as car loans, student loans and other qualifying debt obligations. The exact debts considered by a lender can vary depending on the mortgage program and underwriting rules.

Can I use this calculator to compare different scenarios?

Yes. Change the income, debt payments, down payment, interest rate or loan term and calculate again to compare hypothetical mortgage affordability scenarios.
