What is compound interest?

Compound interest is the process of earning returns on your original investment as well as on returns that have already accumulated. Over time, this can allow your investment to grow at an increasing rate when returns are reinvested.

The effect of compound growth generally becomes more noticeable over longer periods. The amount invested, the rate of return, the frequency of compounding and the time invested can all affect the final value.

How does the compound interest calculator work?

Enter your initial investment, monthly contribution, expected annual return and investment period. The calculator applies the assumed annual return on a monthly basis and adds your monthly contribution to estimate the investment value over time.

The result separates the money you contributed from the estimated compound growth. This helps you understand how much of the final balance comes from your own contributions and how much comes from estimated investment growth.

Why does time matter with compound interest?

Time can have a significant effect on compound growth because returns that remain invested can generate additional returns in later periods. As the investment period becomes longer, this compounding effect can become increasingly important.

Regular contributions can also increase the amount of money that remains invested and potentially benefits from future growth.

Example of compound growth

Suppose you invest $1,000 initially, contribute $100 each month and achieve an average annual return of 7%. If you continue investing for many years and reinvest the returns, the estimated value can become substantially higher than the amount you personally contributed.

This is a hypothetical example. Investment returns can vary from year to year, and past performance or assumed returns do not guarantee future results.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the compound interest formula?

A basic compound interest formula for a lump-sum investment is A = P(1 + r/n)nt, where P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate, n is the number of compounding periods per year and t is the investment period in years.

When regular monthly contributions are included, the calculation also needs to account for the amount and timing of each contribution.

Does this calculator include monthly contributions?

Yes. You can enter a regular monthly contribution to estimate how recurring investments could affect the final value of your portfolio over time.

Does compound interest guarantee investment growth?

No. The calculator uses an assumed annual return to produce an estimate. Actual investment returns can be higher or lower, and investments can also lose value.
