How does a savings calculator work?

A savings calculator estimates how much money you could have in the future by combining your starting balance, regular contributions and an estimated interest rate.

Enter the amount you already have saved, how much you plan to contribute each month, your expected annual interest rate and how long you plan to save. The calculator then estimates your future balance and shows how much comes from your contributions versus estimated interest.

This calculator uses monthly compounding. Interest is added to the balance over time, so future interest can be earned on both your original savings and previously accumulated interest.

Why save regularly?

Making regular contributions can help build savings gradually and allows compound growth to work over longer periods of time.

How to grow your savings

Growing your savings over time usually depends on three main factors: how much you contribute, the return or interest rate you earn, and how long you keep saving.

One of the simplest ways to increase your future savings balance is to make regular contributions. Even a relatively small monthly contribution can add up to a significant amount over several years.

The interest or return earned on your savings can also affect your results. With compound growth, interest can accumulate on both your original savings and previously earned interest.

The amount of time you save can make a major difference as well. Giving your money more time to grow allows regular contributions and compound growth to have a greater effect on your final balance.

Use the calculator above to compare different monthly contributions, interest rates and time periods. This can help you understand how changing each assumption affects the estimated result.

Example: Saving $200 a Month for 10 Years

Suppose you start with $1,000 in savings, contribute $200 every month, and earn an average annual interest rate of 4%. If you continue saving for 10 years, the calculator estimates that your savings could grow to approximately $30,941.

You would contribute a total of $25,000 during the 10-year period: $1,000 from your initial savings plus $24,000 in monthly contributions. The remaining amount would come from estimated interest.

This example illustrates why regular contributions and compound interest can make a significant difference over longer periods. You can change the values above to compare different monthly contributions, interest rates and saving periods.

Actual results may vary depending on the interest rate available, account fees, taxes, inflation and the timing of deposits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much will my savings grow in 10 years?

It depends on your starting balance, monthly contributions and interest rate. Use the calculator above to enter your own numbers and estimate your future savings balance.

Does this savings calculator include compound interest?

Yes. The calculator applies the estimated annual interest rate monthly, allowing your balance to benefit from compound growth over time.

How much should I save each month?

There is no single amount that works for everyone. A practical approach is to choose a monthly contribution that fits your income, expenses and savings goals, and then adjust it as your financial situation changes.

What happens if I increase my monthly contribution?

Increasing your monthly contribution can increase your estimated final balance. The effect can become more significant when you continue saving for many years because your additional contributions may also generate interest over time.

Can I use this calculator for long-term savings?

Yes. You can enter different time periods to compare how regular contributions and compound interest could affect your estimated savings balance over the long term.

Are the results guaranteed?

No. The results are estimates based on the values you enter. Actual savings growth can vary depending on the interest rate, account fees, taxes, inflation and the timing of deposits.
