How Does a Savings Calculator Work?

A savings calculator estimates how much money you could have in the future by combining your starting balance, regular contributions and an assumed interest rate.

Enter the amount you already have saved, how much you plan to contribute each month, your expected annual interest rate and how long you plan to save. The calculator then estimates your future balance and separates your contributions from the estimated interest.

This calculator uses monthly compounding. Interest is added to the balance over time, so future interest can be earned on both your original savings and previously accumulated interest.

Worked Example: Saving $200 Every Month

Suppose you start with $1,000 in savings and deposit $200 at the end of every month. If your savings earn an assumed annual interest rate of 4%, compounded monthly, you can estimate how your balance may develop over a 20-year period.

Over 20 years, you would contribute $49,000 in total: your initial $1,000 plus 240 monthly deposits of $200. Using the assumptions above, the estimated final balance would be approximately $75,577.51, including $26,577.51 in estimated interest.

This example shows the difference between the money you deposit and the additional growth generated by interest. The result is an estimate rather than a guarantee. Actual savings returns may vary, and fees, taxes and inflation can affect the final value and its purchasing power.

To explore other possibilities, change one input at a time. For example, compare a monthly deposit of $150 with one of $250, or compare a 10-year savings period with a 20-year period. This helps you understand how regular deposits, time and the assumed interest rate affect the projection.

How to Read the Savings Growth Chart

The chart compares your projected total savings with the money you have contributed. The purple line represents your estimated account balance, while the blue line shows your initial savings plus your cumulative monthly deposits. The shaded area between the lines represents the estimated interest earned.

As time passes, the gap between the two lines may become larger because interest can accumulate on both your original savings and previously earned interest. The size of this gap depends on the assumed interest rate, your monthly deposits and how long you keep the money invested or saved.

Understanding the Annual Savings Table

The table provides a year-by-year breakdown of your projected savings. Total contributed is the amount you have deposited up to that point, including your initial balance. Interest earned is the difference between the projected balance and your total contributions. Final balance combines both amounts.

Use the table to compare how much of your projected balance comes from your own deposits and how much comes from estimated interest. These figures assume the interest rate remains constant and are not guaranteed. Actual returns may vary, and fees, taxes and inflation can affect the final value.

How to Calculate Savings Growth

Your future savings balance depends mainly on your starting balance, regular contributions, assumed interest rate and the amount of time your money remains in the account.

With compound growth, interest is added to your balance and can subsequently generate additional interest. Regular contributions also increase the amount of money that can potentially earn interest.

Why Save Regularly?

Making regular contributions can help you build savings gradually and gives your money more time to potentially benefit from compound growth.

The amount you contribute each month, the interest rate you earn and the length of time you save can all affect your estimated final balance.

How to Grow Your Savings

Growing your savings over time generally depends on three important factors: how much you contribute, the interest or return you earn and how long you continue saving.

One of the simplest ways to increase your future savings balance is to make regular contributions. Even a relatively small monthly contribution can add up to a substantial amount over several years.

The interest earned on your savings can also affect your results. With compound growth, interest can accumulate on both your original savings and previously earned interest.

Time can make a significant difference as well. Giving your money more time to grow allows regular contributions and compound growth to have a greater effect on your estimated balance.

Example: Saving $200 a Month for 10 Years

Suppose you start with $1,000 in savings, contribute $200 every month and assume an annual interest rate of 4%.

If you continue saving for 10 years, the estimated final balance would be approximately $30,940.79.

You would contribute a total of $25,000: $1,000 from your initial savings plus $24,000 in monthly contributions.

The remaining approximately $5,940.79 would come from estimated interest.

This is a hypothetical example. Actual savings growth can vary depending on the interest rate, account terms, fees, taxes, inflation and the timing of deposits.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the estimated final balance include?

The estimated final balance combines your initial savings, all monthly contributions and the interest accumulated over the selected period. The total amount contributed shows how much money you put in, while estimated interest earned shows the additional growth projected by the calculator.

How much will $200 a month grow in 10 years?

Suppose you start with $1,000, deposit $200 at the end of each month and earn an assumed annual interest rate of 4%, compounded monthly. After 10 years, the estimated balance would be approximately $30,940.79. This includes $25,000 in total contributions and $5,940.79 in estimated interest.

Does this savings calculator include compound interest?

Yes. The calculator applies the annual interest rate as a monthly rate and adds interest to the accumulated balance before adding each month's contribution. This allows previously accumulated interest to contribute to future growth.

What happens if I increase my monthly contribution?

A higher monthly contribution generally increases your projected final balance because you deposit more money and those additional deposits may also earn interest. To compare different plans fairly, keep the starting balance, interest rate and time period unchanged while adjusting the monthly contribution.

Does the calculator account for inflation, fees or taxes?

No. The results are nominal estimates and do not deduct account fees or taxes or adjust for inflation. The future purchasing power of your savings may therefore be lower than the displayed balance suggests.

How much should I save each month?

Choose an amount that fits your income, essential expenses and other financial priorities. You can use the calculator to compare affordable monthly contributions and see how they may affect your savings goal over time.

Are the results guaranteed?

No. The calculator assumes the entered interest rate remains constant throughout the selected period. Actual rates, account conditions and deposit timing may differ, so the projected balance should be treated as an estimate rather than a promise.
